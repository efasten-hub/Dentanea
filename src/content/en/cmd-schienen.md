## Typical symptoms

- Pain in the jaw joint, cheeks or temples
- Clicking or grating in the jaw joint
- Limited mouth opening or a jaw that “catches”
- Grinding or clenching, worn teeth
- Head and neck pain that is worse in the morning
- Snoring or pauses in breathing during sleep

## How the treatment works

1. **Consultation:** we ask about your symptoms, sleep, stress and previous treatments.
2. **Functional examination:** we examine jaw joints and chewing muscles, measure mouth opening and check your bite. If needed, we add imaging, for example an MRI at a radiology practice.
3. **Splint:** after a digital scan, our lab makes a custom occlusal splint (Michigan splint), usually worn at night.
4. **Follow-up:** we adjust the splint in the first weeks and monitor how your symptoms develop.
5. **Additional support:** if needed, we involve physiotherapy, sleep medicine or ENT.

## Anti-snoring splint

A mandibular advancement device holds the lower jaw slightly forward during sleep. This keeps the upper airway more open and can reduce snoring. If sleep apnoea – pauses in breathing during sleep – is suspected, a sleep medicine assessment is needed first. We make the splint in our own lab from a digital scan and adjust it step by step.

## Alternatives and additions

Simple measures often help alongside the splint: relaxation exercises for the chewing muscles, warmth, soft food during acute phases and reducing stress. For sleep apnoea, a breathing mask (CPAP) is the standard treatment; the splint is an alternative if the mask is not tolerated.

## Duration and number of visits

Diagnosis and splint usually take three to four visits over two to three weeks, followed by check-ups as needed.

## Durability and care

Clean the splint every morning with a toothbrush and cold water and keep it dry in its case. Bring it to every check-up. Depending on wear, a splint lasts several years.

## Costs

- **Statutory insurance (GKV):** a simple occlusal splint is covered if medically necessary. A detailed functional analysis is a private service under the German fee scale for dentists (GOZ). An anti-snoring splint for diagnosed obstructive sleep apnoea is covered if a CPAP mask is not an option and a sleep medicine diagnosis is available. [[PRÜFEN: Kassenleistung UKPS]]
- **Private insurance:** charged according to the GOZ. Many policies reimburse functional diagnosis and splints.
