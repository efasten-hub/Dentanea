## Four types of patients

### 1. Statutory health insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung, GKV)

Most people living and working in Germany are insured with a statutory health insurer such as TK, AOK or Barmer. Bring your health card (Gesundheitskarte) to every visit. Check-ups, fillings, root canal treatment, gum treatment and extractions are covered. You do not receive an invoice for these.

### 2. Private health insurance (private Krankenversicherung, PKV)

You receive an invoice according to the German fee scale for dentists (Gebührenordnung für Zahnärzte, GOZ), pay it and claim the amount back from your insurer. How much is reimbursed depends on your policy.

### 3. EU visitors with an EHIC

With a European Health Insurance Card, medically necessary treatment during your stay is generally billed like statutory insurance. Planned treatments such as new crowns or implants are usually not covered.

### 4. International or travel insurance, self-payers

You pay the invoice according to the GOZ and claim it back from your insurer. For larger treatments, ask your insurer for approval of the cost plan in advance.

## Crowns, bridges and dentures: the fixed subsidy

For prosthetics, statutory insurance pays a **fixed subsidy (Festzuschuss)**. It depends on your diagnosis, not on the restoration you choose, and amounts to 60 percent of the cost of the standard solution (Regelversorgung). Before treatment, we prepare a **treatment and cost plan (Heil- und Kostenplan)**, which you submit to your insurer. Treatment starts after approval.

## The bonus booklet (Bonusheft)

Ask for a bonus booklet at your first check-up and have every annual check-up stamped. After five years without gaps, the subsidy rises to 70 percent, after ten years to 75 percent.

## What you usually pay yourself

- Professional teeth cleaning (many statutory insurers pay a subsidy)
- Cosmetic treatments such as veneers, whitening and aligners
- Implants (statutory insurance only subsidises the crown on top)
- Upgrades beyond the standard solution, for example all-ceramic crowns on back teeth

You always receive a written estimate in advance.

## Booking an appointment

You can book online via Doctolib at any time – the booking page is available in English – or call us during opening hours. For a second opinion, bring your existing X-rays and reports, ideally translated or in English.

## What to bring

- Health card (GKV), proof of insurance or EHIC
- Passport or ID for your first visit
- List of your medications and any allergies
- Previous X-rays and dental records, if available

## Emergencies

During opening hours, call us – we try to see patients in pain at short notice. Outside opening hours, the Berlin dental emergency service helps. For life-threatening emergencies, call 112.
