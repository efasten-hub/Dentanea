## How to recognise chalky teeth

- White, yellow or brown patches on the first permanent molars (which come through around age six)
- Often patches on the upper and lower incisors too
- Your child complains of pain when brushing, with cold or when eating
- Parts of the enamel crumble away shortly after the tooth comes through

With MIH, the enamel is not sufficiently mineralised – it is softer and more porous than normal. This has nothing to do with poor brushing.

## How common is MIH?

MIH is common. In the Fifth German Oral Health Study, almost 30 percent of 12-year-olds showed signs of MIH, mostly mild. Severe forms with enamel breakdown are much rarer. [[PRÜFEN: aktuelle Zahl aus DMS 6]]

## What helps?

Treatment depends on how badly the teeth are affected:

1. **Mild forms:** fluoride varnish, a higher-fluoride toothpaste (after consultation), sealing the biting surfaces
2. **Moderate forms:** composite or glass ionomer fillings to cover sensitive areas
3. **Severe forms:** metal or resin crowns protect badly affected molars. Sometimes it makes sense to remove a severely damaged molar in coordination with the orthodontist.
4. **Incisors:** discolouration can be improved with composite or lightening techniques.

## Tips for home

- Brush twice a day with fluoride children’s toothpaste in the age-appropriate amount.
- If brushing hurts, use a soft toothbrush and lukewarm water.
- Avoid sugary drinks and snacks between meals.
- Check-ups every three to six months, depending on severity.

## What insurance covers

For children and teenagers aged 6 to 17, German statutory insurance covers individual prevention, such as fluoride and sealing of the molars. Fillings are covered. Crowns are subsidised. More elaborate cosmetic restorations may involve additional costs under the German fee scale for dentists (GOZ). We discuss this with you beforehand.
