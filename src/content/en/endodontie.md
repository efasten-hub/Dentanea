## When is root canal treatment needed?

When decay, a crack or an accident reaches the dental pulp (the soft tissue inside the tooth), it becomes inflamed or dies. Typical signs are severe throbbing pain, pain when biting, long-lasting sensitivity to cold or heat, or swelling. Sometimes the inflammation causes no symptoms and is only seen on an X-ray.

## How the treatment works

1. **Diagnosis:** examination, vitality test and a digital X-ray.
2. **Anaesthetic and isolation:** a rubber dam keeps the tooth dry and free of saliva.
3. **Cleaning:** we open the tooth, remove the tissue and shape the canals with fine rotary instruments. An electronic device measures the length of the canals.
4. **Disinfection:** repeated irrigation with disinfecting solutions reduces the bacteria in the branched canal system.
5. **Filling:** the canals are sealed tightly, and an X-ray checks the result.
6. **Sealing:** the tooth receives a tight filling, later a crown if needed.

## Alternatives

- **Re-treatment:** treating a previously root-filled tooth again
- **Apicoectomy:** surgical removal of the root tip if inflammation persists
- **Extraction:** followed by an implant, bridge or resin-bonded bridge

## Duration and number of visits

Usually one to three visits of 60 to 90 minutes each.

## Durability and care

A well root-filled and tightly restored tooth can be kept for many years. A tight filling or crown, good oral hygiene and a follow-up X-ray after about a year are important.

## Costs

- **Statutory insurance (GKV):** root canal treatment is covered if the tooth is considered worth saving; special conditions apply to molars. Procedures beyond the statutory benefit – such as rotary instrumentation with special file systems or additional irrigation protocols – are private services, agreed in writing in advance.
