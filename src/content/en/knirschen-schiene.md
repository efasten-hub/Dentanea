## What is bruxism?

Experts distinguish two forms:

- **Awake bruxism:** clenching or grinding during the day, often unconsciously when concentrating or stressed
- **Sleep bruxism:** rhythmic grinding or clenching during sleep

Both are widespread. At first they are not a disease but a behaviour. Treatment becomes necessary when consequences appear.

## Typical signs

- Tense or painful chewing muscles, temple pain or headaches in the morning
- Flattened or chipped tooth edges, cracks in the enamel
- Sensitive teeth, tooth marks on the tongue or cheeks
- Clicking or painful jaw joints
- Your partner hears grinding at night

## Causes

Bruxism usually has several causes at once. Stress and tension, sleep disorders, certain medications (for example some antidepressants), alcohol, nicotine and caffeine play a role. Faulty tooth contacts used to be considered the main cause; this is now considered disproven. That is why we do not grind teeth down prematurely.

## What helps?

1. **Awareness:** notice during the day whether you clench. At rest, teeth should not touch – “lips together, teeth apart”.
2. **Relaxation:** exercises for jaw and neck, warmth, progressive muscle relaxation, stress reduction.
3. **Occlusal splint:** protects the teeth from wear and distributes forces. It is made individually from a digital scan and usually worn at night.
4. **Physiotherapy:** for tense muscles or limited mouth opening.
5. **Sleep medicine:** if there is also snoring or pauses in breathing.

## What a splint cannot do

A splint does not remove the cause of grinding. It protects and relieves – like a helmet that protects your head without preventing falls. That is why relaxation and, if needed, stress management are always part of it.

German statutory insurance covers a simple occlusal splint if medically necessary. A detailed functional analysis is a private service under the German fee scale for dentists (GOZ). More about treatment on the page [TMD & splints](/en/services/tmd-splint-therapy/).
