## The situation

A single missing tooth – for example after an accident, because it never developed or after inflammation – can be replaced in different ways. The traditional bridge used to be standard: both neighbouring teeth are drilled down and carry the replacement tooth. Today there are two solutions that spare healthy neighbouring teeth: the **implant** and the **resin-bonded bridge** (also called adhesive or Maryland bridge).

## The implant

An implant is an artificial tooth root made of titanium or ceramic. It is placed in the jawbone and, after healing, carries a crown.

### Advantages

- Neighbouring teeth stay untouched.
- The bone is loaded and shrinks less.
- Suitable for front and back teeth.

### Disadvantages

- Surgery is required.
- Enough bone is needed, otherwise bone grafting.
- Only possible once growth is complete, usually not before 18.
- Treatment takes several months.
- Usually the more expensive option.

## The resin-bonded bridge

The replacement tooth is attached to a thin ceramic wing that is bonded to the back of a neighbouring tooth. Studies of single-wing all-ceramic bridges for front teeth show very good long-term results.

### Advantages

- No surgery, no bone grafting
- Neighbouring teeth are drilled very little or not at all.
- Quick, usually finished in two to three weeks
- Also suitable for teenagers whose jaws are still growing
- Usually less expensive than an implant

### Disadvantages

- Mainly suitable for incisors
- The supporting tooth must be healthy and large enough.
- It can come loose – but can usually be bonded again.
- The bone beneath the gap is not loaded.

## Which suits whom?

| Situation | Implant | Resin-bonded bridge |
|---|---|---|
| Teenagers still growing | – | ✓ |
| Missing incisor, healthy neighbours | ✓ | ✓ |
| Missing back tooth | ✓ | selected cases only |
| Too little bone, no grafting wanted | – | ✓ |
| Neighbouring teeth heavily filled or crowned | ✓ | – |

## How to decide

Ask for both options to be explained – with an honest assessment of the pros and cons in your case and a cost comparison. Important factors are:

1. Your age and whether your jaw is still growing
2. Condition of the neighbouring teeth and gums
3. Available bone (X-ray)
4. Space and bite
5. Your wishes regarding treatment time, surgery and costs

## Costs and insurance

German statutory insurance pays a **fixed subsidy (Festzuschuss)** based on the diagnosis, not on the solution you choose. It amounts to 60 percent of the standard solution, with a complete **bonus booklet** 70 or 75 percent. For implants, the insurer usually only subsidises the crown, not the implant itself. You see your **own share** in advance in the cost plan. More in [What does dental prosthetics cost?](/en/guide/cost-of-dental-prosthetics/)
