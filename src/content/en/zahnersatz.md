## Which restoration for whom?

- **Partial crown:** when part of a tooth is lost, for example after a large filling. It only replaces what is missing and preserves healthy tooth structure.
- **Crown:** when a tooth is badly damaged or needs protection after root canal treatment. The crown covers the tooth like a cap.
- **Bridge:** closes a gap by attaching to the neighbouring teeth. Useful when those teeth need crowns anyway.
- **Resin-bonded bridge:** replaces a single tooth without drilling down the neighbouring teeth. See [resin-bonded bridge](/en/services/resin-bonded-bridge/).
- **Partial and full dentures:** removable restorations when several or all teeth are missing, held by clasps, telescopic crowns or implants.
- **Implant-supported restorations:** crowns, bridges or dentures on artificial tooth roots. See [dental implants](/en/services/dental-implants/).

## How the treatment works

1. **Examination and advice:** we check teeth, gums and bite and take X-rays if needed, then discuss the options.
2. **Plan and costs:** you receive a treatment and cost plan (Heil- und Kostenplan). With statutory insurance, it is submitted to your insurer before treatment starts.
3. **Preparation:** the tooth is prepared under local anaesthetic and scanned digitally. A temporary restoration protects it in the meantime.
4. **Made in our own lab:** our master dental technician makes the restoration. If needed, we match shade and shape directly at the chair.
5. **Fitting and check:** we check fit and bite carefully and fix the restoration. A follow-up a few weeks later makes sure everything is right.

## Alternatives

Not every damaged tooth needs a crown. Sometimes a large filling or a partial crown is enough. If a tooth is missing, options include a bridge, a resin-bonded bridge, an implant or a partial denture. Sometimes leaving a gap is acceptable. We explain what speaks for and against each solution in your case.

## Duration and number of visits

A crown or small bridge usually takes two to three visits over two to three weeks. A denture needs four to six visits. Extensive treatments are planned in stages.

## Durability and care

How long a restoration lasts depends mainly on your oral hygiene, your bite and regular check-ups. Brush twice a day and clean between your teeth, for example with interdental brushes. Special floss helps under bridges. We recommend professional cleaning once or twice a year. If you grind your teeth at night, a splint protects your restorations.

## Costs

- **Statutory insurance (GKV):** your insurer pays a **fixed subsidy (Festzuschuss)**. It depends on the diagnosis, not on the restoration you choose, and covers 60 percent of the cost of the standard solution for your diagnosis (Regelversorgung).
- **Bonus booklet (Bonusheft):** if you can show an annual check-up for the past five years, the subsidy rises to 70 percent, after ten years to 75 percent. Low-income patients can apply for a hardship rule.
- **Your own share (Eigenanteil):** you pay the rest. Choosing a more elaborate solution than the standard one, such as all-ceramic crowns on back teeth, increases your share. Part of the bill is then charged according to the German fee scale for dentists (GOZ).
- **Private insurance and self-payers:** charged according to the GOZ. How much is reimbursed depends on your policy. Please submit the cost plan to your insurer in advance.

**New to Germany?** The fixed subsidy only applies with German statutory insurance. With an international or travel policy you usually pay the bill and claim it back. Read more in [Seeing a dentist in Berlin](/en/guide/dentist-berlin-german-system/).

As a guide, your own share for an all-ceramic crown is usually around [[PREIS]] euros. Your cost plan shows the exact amount for your situation.
